Career evaluation and model evidence

The user selected healthcare data analysts in NAICS 62 and explicitly authorized local computation instead of the course’s assigned EC2 environment. Results below were generated from the local Jobs_2026 snapshot on October 3, 2026 (Jobs_2026 2026). The feature design adapts Goindani et al. (2017), using scikit-learn estimators (Pedregosa et al. 2011).

The pipeline read 2,350,355 rows in 97 posting partitions from the local snapshot (Jobs_2026 2026) and selected January–September 2026 observations. All input files have recorded SHA-256 hashes. No posting ID appeared twice in this input snapshot.

Filter stage Remaining postings

Unknown country and unknown sector are exclusions, not negative industry labels. Staffing status that is missing is retained. The geographic and sector filters remove much of the source data, so the retained sample is not representative of all Jobs_2026 records or all US hiring.

1 Employer sample and split

There are 24,214 employer-name groups before employer-level filters. Of these, 22,438 have fewer than 20 eligible postings. A further 1,414 lack a sector covering at least 80% of their postings. The model therefore uses 362 employers, below the paper’s 10,000-employer target; 85 have exactly consistent sector labels.

The split uses seed 2017 and stratification, with zero shared employer-name keys across partitions. It is 70/10/20 up to integer rounding. Exact company-name case and whitespace normalization is used; aliases and subsidiaries may still appear as separate entities. This is weaker than the paper’s proprietary entity normalization.

These metrics measure agreement with dataset-derived sector labels, not independently verified employer truth. GBDT misses 15 of the 34 healthcare employers, with 0 false positives among 39 other employers. This small sample cannot establish perfect precision in deployment. The all-positive baseline has F1 0.636, compared with GBDT’s 0.717; ranking models on F1 alone would conceal their very different precision and recall.

For a career product, predicted healthcare labels could narrow an employer-review queue, but the missed healthcare employers would make automatic exclusion costly. Retain existing labels and review disagreements before changing the career sample. No manually adjudicated undefined-employer labels are available, so the paper’s utility scores cannot be reproduced here.

3 Training and feature details

Vocabulary thresholds are learned from the training employers only. The selected vocabulary contains 431 title candidates and 12 name words. After the 1% within-employer filter, the fitted matrix has 258 observed columns; candidates absent from all training feature vectors are dropped.

The linear SVM tests C = 0.1, 1 and 10 with balanced class penalties. GBDT tests depths 2 and 3 with 100 estimators and learning rate 0.1. Each model is chosen by validation F1, then evaluated on the test partition without refitting. Exact configurations, validation scores, versions and full classification reports are in run.json.

4 Differences from the paper

The reference design is Goindani et al. (2017); the implementation uses scikit-learn (Pedregosa et al. 2011).

This is an exploratory adaptation: a 2026 dataset, one industry, US-only observations, scikit-learn LinearSVC and GBDT, validation-based tuning instead of the paper’s reported cross-validation, and normalized employer-name unigrams without all raw name variants. Name-word frequency counts employers containing the word. The 80% dominant-sector rule is an explicit adaptation for inconsistent posting-level labels. A preliminary unrestricted-year, exact-consistency run exposed data-quality problems; its results are superseded. The final study is not a preregistered confirmatory replication.

Industry fields may themselves derive from title or employer heuristics. Their provenance has not been independently audited. Label circularity, unresolved aliases, cohort selection and noisy titles limit the meaning of predictive agreement. A temporal holdout and independent label review remain future validation work.

5 Keep industry confidence separate from opportunity quality

Industry classification helps determine whether a posting belongs in the selected analytical sample. It does not measure whether the job is desirable, accessible or well matched to a student. Records with disputed employer identity should enter a review queue before contributing to sector-level advice.

6 Proposed transparent scorecard

A course implementation could compare role segments using the following dimensions. These weights are illustrative design choices and have not been validated with users or data.

Dimension Proposed weight Evidence
Demand 25% Deduplicated posting volume in a fixed period
Salary 20% Comparable observed salary distributions
Entry accessibility 20% Share with entry-level requirements
Skill fit 25% Assessed profile against explicit skill targets
Remote flexibility 10% Share explicitly remote

Normalize the dimension scores to 0–1 within a declared comparison set, then compute their weighted sum. Skill fit and accessibility together receive 45% to reflect the proposed student audience. This rationale is a preference choice, not an empirical result. Show the component values alongside the total.

7 Missingness and sensitivity

Do not rank a segment when a required component is missing. Report coverage and the reason for withholding its score. If all compared values for a dimension are equal, that dimension contains no ranking information; declare a neutral common score. Repeat rankings under equal weights and changes of ±10 percentage points to major weights, renormalizing to sum to 1. Report rank changes instead of presenting one weighting as objectively correct.

8 Independent validation still needed

Use an employer-disjoint holdout and independently reviewed labels. Report precision, recall, F1, class support and a confusion matrix separately for each industry. Review a sample of both agreement and disagreement records. A fair comparison should include the legacy label system and simple baselines. Document how many review flags are useful and the analyst time required.

Current status: the Jobs_2026 study fits and evaluates a linear SVM and GBDT, including confusion matrices and simple baselines. It uses dataset-derived labels without independent adjudication. Market benchmarks have been calculated, but the weighted career scorecard remains uncomputed because personal ratings and sufficient salary evidence are unavailable. Classification is the completed Module 5 analytical track.
